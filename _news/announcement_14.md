I gave a talk on **Polyhedral Theory for Integer Programming** on the opening day of the [LSO Advanced School 2026](https://iitr.ac.in/lso/) at IIT Roorkee (Sept 30 – Oct 4, 2026). This is the first in a new series of schools on advanced topics in large-scale optimization, following the introductory LSO Summer Schools. This edition focused on cutting planes and column generation.

The talk covered:

- Polyhedra as feasible sets of LPs and IPs, and the convex hull of integer points
- When the convex hull of integer points is a polyhedron: Meyer's theorem and the role of rationality
- The resolution and Minkowski–Weyl theorems
- Comparing formulations by strength, and dominant and redundant valid inequalities
- Dimension, faces and facets of polyhedra, and why facets are necessary
- Proving a formulation gives the convex hull, via total unimodularity and total dual integrality
- Proving an inequality is facet-defining, with minimal cover inequalities for the binary knapsack set as the running example

It closed with a question on lifting minimal cover inequalities, which led into the sessions on valid inequalities and branch-and-cut that followed the next day.
